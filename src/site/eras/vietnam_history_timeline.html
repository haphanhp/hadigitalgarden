Timeline of Vietnamese History

From legendary origins to the modern era

🐉 2879–258 BCE Hồng Bàng Period (Legendary Era)

Semi-legendary founding period of Vietnamese civilization, attributed to the Hùng Kings and the Văn Lang kingdom.

  • c. 2879 BCE Traditional founding date of Văn Lang, Vietnam's first kingdom, by the Hùng Kings.
  • c. 7th century BCE Đông Sơn culture flourishes, known for bronze drums and early metallurgy.
  • 258 BCE An Dương Vương conquers Văn Lang, founding the Âu Lạc kingdom.
🏯 207 BCE – 938 CE Chinese Domination (Bắc Thuộc)

Over a millennium of intermittent Chinese rule, punctuated by major rebellions that shaped Vietnamese national identity.

  • 207 BCE Triệu Đà establishes Nam Việt, later annexed by the Han Dynasty in 111 BCE.
  • 40 CE Trưng Sisters (Hai Bà Trưng) lead a rebellion against Han rule, briefly establishing independence.
  • 248 CE Lady Triệu (Bà Triệu) leads another major uprising against Chinese forces.
  • 544–602 Lý Bí founds the short-lived Vạn Xuân kingdom (Early Lý Dynasty).
  • 938 Ngô Quyền defeats Southern Han forces at the Battle of Bạch Đằng River, ending Chinese domination.
👑 939 – 1009 Early Independence: Ngô, Đinh, and Early Lê Dynasties

Establishment of independent Vietnamese statehood after centuries of foreign rule.

  • 939 Ngô Quyền proclaims himself king, founding the Ngô Dynasty.
  • 968 Đinh Bộ Lĩnh unifies the country, founds Đại Việt Cồ Việt and the Đinh Dynasty.
  • 980 Lê Hoàn founds the Early Lê Dynasty, repelling a Song Chinese invasion.
📜 1009 – 1225 Lý Dynasty

A golden age of cultural and political consolidation, considered the first long-lasting stable dynasty.

  • 1010 Lý Thái Tổ moves the capital to Thăng Long (modern Hanoi).
  • 1054 The kingdom is renamed Đại Việt.
  • 1070 Founding of the Temple of Literature (Văn Miếu), Vietnam's first national university.
  • 1075–1077 Lý Thường Kiệt repels a Song Chinese invasion in a notable preemptive campaign.
⚔️ 1225 – 1400 Trần Dynasty

Famous for repelling three Mongol invasions and developing a distinct Vietnamese cultural identity.

  • 1258, 1285, 1288 Trần forces, led by Trần Hưng Đạo, defeat three Mongol invasions, including the decisive Battle of Bạch Đằng (1288).
  • 1306 Princess Huyền Trân's marriage leads to the cession of Châu Ô and Châu Lý (Huế region) to Đại Việt.
  • 1400 Hồ Quý Ly usurps the throne, founding the short-lived Hồ Dynasty.
🗡️ 1407 – 1427 Ming Occupation and the Lam Sơn Uprising

A brief but harsh period of Chinese Ming rule, ended by a decade-long guerrilla resistance.

  • 1407 Ming China conquers Đại Việt following the fall of the Hồ Dynasty.
  • 1418 Lê Lợi launches the Lam Sơn uprising from Thanh Hóa province.
  • 1427 Ming forces are decisively defeated; Nguyễn Trãi drafts the "Great Proclamation" (Bình Ngô đại cáo).
📚 1428 – 1789 Later Lê Dynasty and Civil Wars

A long dynasty marked by a cultural golden age under Lê Thánh Tông, followed by centuries of de facto division between rival lords.

  • 1428 Lê Lợi founds the Later Lê Dynasty as Emperor Lê Thái Tổ.
  • 1460–1497 Reign of Lê Thánh Tông; major legal, administrative, and territorial expansion (including the Hồng Đức legal code).
  • 1471 Conquest of the Champa capital Vijaya, accelerating southward expansion (Nam tiến).
  • 1533–1789 Lê emperors become largely symbolic; real power split between the Trịnh lords (north) and Nguyễn lords (south), separated roughly by the Gianh River.
  • 1620s–1770s Continued Nam tiến brings the Mekong Delta under Vietnamese control.
🌾 1771 – 1802 Tây Sơn Dynasty

A peasant-led rebellion that briefly reunified the country and repelled foreign invasions.

  • 1771 The Tây Sơn brothers (Nguyễn Nhạc, Nguyễn Huệ, Nguyễn Lữ) launch their uprising.
  • 1785 Nguyễn Huệ defeats a Siamese invasion at the Battle of Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút.
  • 1789 Nguyễn Huệ (as Emperor Quang Trung) defeats a Qing Chinese invasion at the Battle of Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa.
  • 1792 Death of Quang Trung; the dynasty weakens rapidly afterward.
🏛️ 1802 – 1945 Nguyễn Dynasty

The last imperial dynasty, which unified the country under one ruler for the first time in centuries before falling under French control.

  • 1802 Nguyễn Ánh defeats the Tây Sơn, becomes Emperor Gia Long, names the country "Việt Nam," establishes Huế as the capital.
  • 1858 France attacks Đà Nẵng, beginning its military intervention in Vietnam.
  • 1862 Treaty of Saigon cedes parts of southern Vietnam (Cochinchina) to France.
  • 1883–1884 Treaties of Huế establish French protectorates over Annam and Tonkin.
🇫🇷 1887 – 1954 French Colonial Period (Indochina)

Vietnam administered as part of French Indochina, with the Nguyễn emperors retained as figureheads. A period of growing nationalist and communist resistance movements.

  • 1887 French Indochina is formally established, uniting Vietnam, Cambodia, and (later) Laos.
  • 1930 Hồ Chí Minh founds the Indochinese Communist Party.
  • 1940–1945 Japan occupies Vietnam during World War II while nominal French Vichy administration continues.
  • 1945 Famine kills an estimated one to two million people in northern Vietnam.
  • August 1945 The August Revolution; Bảo Đại abdicates, ending the Nguyễn Dynasty.
  • September 2, 1945 Hồ Chí Minh declares independence, founding the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi — its flag a yellow star on a red field.
  • 1946–1954 First Indochina War between France and the Việt Minh.
  • 1954 Battle of Điện Biên Phủ ends in decisive Việt Minh victory; Geneva Accords temporarily divide Vietnam at the 17th parallel.
🇻🇳 1954 – 1975 Divided Vietnam and the Vietnam War

Vietnam split into a communist North (red flag with a yellow star) and an anti-communist South, the Republic of Vietnam (yellow flag with three red stripes), leading to a prolonged and devastating war that drew in major international powers.

  • 1955 Ngô Đình Diệm proclaims the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam) in Saigon, flying the yellow flag with three red stripes.
  • 1960 Formation of the National Liberation Front (Việt Cộng) in the South.
  • 1964 Gulf of Tonkin incident leads to escalation of direct U.S. military involvement.
  • 1968 Tet Offensive marks a major turning point in the war and U.S. public opinion.
  • 1973 Paris Peace Accords lead to withdrawal of U.S. combat troops.
  • April 30, 1975 Fall of Saigon; North Vietnamese forces capture the city, ending the war.
🇻🇳 1976 – Present Reunification and Đổi Mới Era

A unified socialist republic, flying the red flag with a yellow star, that undergoes major economic reform and reintegration into the global economy.

  • 1976 Official reunification; the country is renamed the Socialist Republic of Vietnam, with Saigon renamed Ho Chi Minh City.
  • 1978–1989 Vietnam's military involvement in Cambodia leads to international isolation and a brief border war with China (1979).
  • 1986 Đổi Mới ("Renovation") economic reforms launched, shifting toward a market-oriented economy.
  • 1995 Normalization of diplomatic relations with the United States; Vietnam joins ASEAN.
  • 2007 Vietnam joins the World Trade Organization (WTO).
  • 2010s–2020s Continued rapid economic growth, deepening international integration, and ongoing political and social change.
